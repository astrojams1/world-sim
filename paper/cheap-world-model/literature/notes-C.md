## (b) What "world model" means in the literature, and how we should scope our use of the term

Quoted definitions (verbatim from the resolved records):

1. **ha2018world** (abstract): "Our world model can be trained quickly in an unsupervised manner to learn a compressed spatial and temporal representation of the environment." In the body the world model is the pair V (VAE, "compresses what it sees into a small representative code") + M (MDN-RNN, "a predictive model of the future z vectors"), and the paper explicitly says "most of our agent's complexity resides in the world model (V and M)". They open with Forrester's mental-model quote: "The image of the world around us, which we carry in our head, is just a model."

2. **chen2026definition** (Definition 2.1): "A world model is a compression modeling of the state transition processes of the physical world, constructed under the constraints of finite computational resources." The same paper then says the term "is used in two partially overlapping but philosophically distinct senses (Ding et al., 2024). In the first, a world model is primarily an instrument for understanding: it compresses sensory data into stable internal representations that expose the entities, relations, and mechanisms needed to interpret the present situation ... In the second, a world model is primarily an instrument for prediction".

3. **wang2026world** (Sec. II, operational definition for manipulation): "A world model is a predictive system, learned end-to-end or assembled from learned and analytic components, that estimates how task-relevant aspects of the external world evolve over time and, in robotic manipulation, predicts or evaluates that evolution under robot intervention." And: "A static visual encoder, object detector, segmentation model, or language-conditioned perception module may provide inputs to a world model, but it is not itself a world model unless it models temporal change."

4. **ding2024understanding** (Sec. 1): "The definition of a world model remains a subject of ongoing debate, generally divided into two primary perspectives: understanding the world and predicting the future ... early work by Ha and Schmidhuber focused on abstracting the external world to gain a deep understanding of its underlying mechanisms. In contrast, LeCun argued that a world model should not only perceive and model the real world but also possess the capacity to envision possible future states".

5. **nvidia2025cosmos** (abstract): "Physical AI needs to be trained digitally first. It needs a digital twin of itself, the policy model, and a digital twin of the world, the world model."

What this means for us:

- The dominant meaning (Ha & Schmidhuber, Dreamer, V-JEPA 2, Cosmos, all three 2025-26 surveys) is a *learned, action-conditioned predictive model*. Under wang2026world's definition our system is explicitly *not* a world model; it is a perception/state-estimation module. Under chen2026definition it sits at the "understanding-oriented" edge (state estimation is listed among the objectives of that branch). kong20253d and chen2026definition both note the term is contested and inconsistently used, which we can cite to justify a careful scoping sentence rather than a claim.
- What we actually produce is closer to what the real-to-sim literature calls a **digital twin** (dai2024automated, huang2026synctwin, chen2026agentic, ranawaka2026simfoundry) or a **simulation-ready scene description** (chen2024urdformer, mandi2024real2code). Cosmos's phrasing "a digital twin of the world, the world model" and Goal-VLA's "object state representation is the golden interface" give us licence to connect the two, but only if we say that the *dynamics* come from the physics engine, not from us.
- Recommended framing: use "world model" only in the compound "explicit world model" / "world-model state" and define it on first use; use "digital twin" or "scene state" everywhere else; avoid claiming a "learned" or "predictive" world model.

Recommended sentence for the paper (abstract or intro, adapt as needed):

> "We use *world model* in the state-estimation sense — an explicit, simulator-ready description of the objects in a scene (shape, colour, size, pose) — rather than the learned latent-dynamics sense of Ha & Schmidhuber [ha2018world] and Dreamer [hafner2023mastering]; the resulting scene is a digital twin whose dynamics are supplied by a physics engine, so it complements rather than replaces predictive world models [wang2026world, chen2026definition]."

Safer title/abstract wording options: "explicit world-model state", "simulator-ready scene state", "cheap digital twin from two views". If the title keeps "world model", put "explicit" or "state" next to it, and the first sentence of the abstract should contain the disambiguation above.

## (c) Closest real-to-sim works

**Automated Creation of Digital Cousins (ACDC) [dai2024automated]** is the nearest in spirit: a fully automated, training-free, VLM-orchestrated pipeline (GPT-4o for captions and mounted/unmounted decisions, GroundedSAM-v2, Depth-Anything-v2, DINOv2/CLIP retrieval) from a single RGB image to a physically plausible interactive scene, with reported wall-clock (about 7 s/object extraction, 20 s/object matching, <30 s scene assembly) but no dollar cost. It needs camera intrinsics and monocular depth, retrieves the nearest library asset rather than estimating shape/size, and reports sim-to-sim geometric fidelity of 4-16 cm centre error and 0.03-0.12 rad orientation error on 6-15-object scenes. We should compare against it directly: uncalibrated two-view input, explicit size/rotation output, sub-second per-object cost, and our per-scene accuracy.

**URDFormer [chen2024urdformer]** and **Real2Code [mandi2024real2code]** are the two "image -> simulation description" precedents that use learned inverse models. URDFormer maps a single RGB image to a URDF (categories, parent links, discretised placement, part kinematics) with a ViT/transformer trained on diffusion-generated paired data; accuracy is reported as category/parent accuracy and discretised spatial error, plus a 78% real-robot success rate after real2sim2real. Real2Code takes a handful of multi-view RGB (DUSt3R) or RGB-D images of an articulated object, fine-tunes SAM, a shape-completion net and CodeLlama-7B, and emits MJCF joint code; its zero-shot GPT-4 baseline underperforms the fine-tuned model, which is a useful contrast for our "hosted LLM + classical tools, no fine-tuning" result. Neither reports latency or cost.

**Agentic Real2Sim [chen2026agentic]** is the closest *methodologically*: a VLM agent orchestrates deterministic geometry tools (SAM 3, SAM 3D, FoundationStereo, FoundationPose) and is queried only for bounded, schema-constrained decisions; they evaluate four backends and report total model bills ($2.62 for 100 DROID episodes with Gemma-4-31B; GPT-5.4 costs 31.4x more) with replay success below 50%. Their input is far richer than ours (calibrated synchronized cameras, depth/stereo, robot trajectory), and they target episode replay rather than static state, but their cost-reporting convention (per-episode model bill, backend comparison) is the one we should mirror. SimFoundry [ranawaka2026simfoundry] (video -> zero-shot twin, 0.911 sim-real correlation) and SyncTwin [huang2026synctwin] (VGGT assets + ICP synchronisation) are worth a sentence each as concurrent digital-twin systems; xiang2026real is the reference for physics-consistent pose refinement from RGB-D if we discuss extending render-and-compare with contact constraints.

Gaps none of these fill (our contribution statement): two *uncalibrated* views with self-calibration from the room silhouette; explicit per-object size and Euler rotation; end-to-end per-scene dollar cost; no training or fine-tuning of any component.
